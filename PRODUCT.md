# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Primary:** A dedicated AV team operating PTZ cameras during sign language congregation meetings. They are technically familiar operators who manage camera positions for both preparation and live events.

**Context:** The tool is used in a church/meeting hall setting where multiple camera angles need to support sign language interpretation — switching between the interpreter, speaker, congregation, and other key positions during meetings. Operators may be managing the camera system while also handling other AV responsibilities.

## Product Purpose

SC PTZ Control exists to give a sign language congregation precise, reliable control over PTZ cameras through their DVR/NVR system. It solves two workflows equally:
- **Pre-configuration:** Operators set up and refine 24–100 camera presets (positions) before meetings, capturing snapshots for visual reference.
- **Live switching:** During meetings, operators quickly jump between preset camera angles with confidence, using the seat map for complex auditorium layouts.

Success looks like: the camera always lands on the right position, the operator never second-guesses which preset is which, and the interface stays out of the way during live moments.

## Brand Personality

**Clean, reliable, precise.** This is a professional tool for a real job — it should feel trustworthy and focused, not playful or flashy. It's the kind of tool that earns its place in the taskbar because it works every time.

- **Clean:** Uncluttered, with clear visual hierarchy. Every element earns its space.
- **Reliable:** Solid, predictable interactions. Feedback is immediate and unambiguous.
- **Precise:** Controls feel exact. The seat map, preset grid, and settings all communicate exact state.

## Anti-references

- Over-designed "creative" dashboards with decorative elements, gradients, or heavy illustrations.
- Cluttered broadcast-style UIs with unnecessary knobs, meters, or skeuomorphic controls.
- Generic SaaS templates that feel impersonal or corporate.
- Anything that feels like it was "designed by AI" — no glassmorphism, gradient text, wide shadowed cards, or decorative grid backgrounds.

## Design Principles

1. **Glanceable operations.** During live meetings, operators scan, not read. Critical state (current preset, seat assignments, capture progress) must be immediately recognizable from a glance. Typography, color, and spacing all serve this.

2. **Reliability as a feature.** In a live event context, every interaction must feel solid. Actions give clear feedback. Errors surface gracefully with actionable messages. Destructive actions require confirmation. State persists across sessions.

3. **Expert confidence.** Designed for operators who know what they're doing. The interface assumes competence: shortcuts are available, advanced features are surfaced (not hidden behind "show more"), and the tool never second-guesses the user.

4. **Purpose-before-polish.** Every visual decision serves the operational goal. Dark mode is the default because the tool is used in a low-light meeting environment. Cards are purposeful containers, not decorative. Motion is functional (transition feedback), not ornamental.

5. **Complete state visibility.** At any moment, the operator should know: what camera position is active, which presets have snapshots, what the seat map looks like, and whether the device is connected. No hidden state, no surprises.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard accessibility practices apply. The tool is used in a meeting hall with varying ambient light — dark mode is default for low-light comfort. Contrast ratios follow WCAG AA guidelines for body text (≥4.5:1). The UI is navigable by keyboard for operators who prefer it.
